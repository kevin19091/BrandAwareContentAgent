Source: Fabindia brand/heritage coverage; Creative Brands Mag, Latterly.org,
Marketing Monk, 4AM World, Osum, 2026 analyses.

Heritage: founded 1960 by John Bissell to connect India's handwoven textile
and craft traditions with a modern retail audience. Today works with 55,000+
artisans and 12,000+ farmers through community-based enterprises.

Positioning: blends old and new — traditional Indian craftsmanship read
through a contemporary lifestyle lens, not a museum-piece framing of
heritage. Core strength is authenticity: products carry a real, traceable
story of the artisans and materials behind them, not a generic "ethnic wear"
label.

Voice: warm, rooted, unhurried — closer to storytelling about people and
place than to fast-fashion urgency. Sustainability is structural (natural
materials, long-life design, fair artisan livelihoods) rather than a
marketing overlay.

Audience: middle-to-upper-class Indian consumers plus diaspora/international
buyers seeking authentic, sustainably made Indian goods — currently also
expanding into modern western wear (the "Fabels" line) without abandoning
the heritage-craft core.

Pillars: artisan authenticity, tradition-meets-contemporary, sustainability
as structure not slogan, storytelling over hard-sell.
